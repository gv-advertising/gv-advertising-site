Exhibit B — The wording regulators quote back to you

The five phrases that trigger FDA letters

Most of the 2026 warning letters to GLP-1 marketers cite the same handful of sentences — word for word, from the marketer's own site or ads. If any of these appear in your copy, product photos or intake flow, you are already on the list.

  1. “Same active ingredient as Wegovy / Ozempic / Mounjaro / Zepbound”
  2. “Generic semaglutide” or “generic alternative”
  3. Your own brand name on the vial label in product photos
  4. “FDA-approved” attached to the ingredient, the pharmacy or the program
  5. “Clinically proven” results borrowed from brand-name trials

Anticipated objections

GLP-1 marketing questions.

Can you get a restricted Meta or Google ad account running again?

Often, yes — but the appeal is the smaller half of the job. Accounts get restricted because the copy, the landing page or the tracking tripped a health policy. We fix the asset that caused the flag first, document the change, then file the appeal with the corrected creative attached. If the account is unrecoverable we rebuild on a clean structure and carry the compliant assets over.

Do you write the ads or review the ones we already have?

Both. The Compliance Redline reviews what you are running today and marks every flagged line against the rule it breaks. The retainers add creative written from scratch to pass review, plus unlimited redline of anything your team writes.

Is this legal advice?

No. GV Advertising is a marketing firm. We build inside the constraints regulators and ad platforms have already published, avoid the enforcement triggers documented in FDA warning letters and FTC actions, and write down why. Your counsel signs off on legal questions.

Can we say our compounded semaglutide has the same active ingredient as Wegovy or Ozempic?

We do not write it. That comparison is the most-cited sentence in the 2026 FDA warning letters to telehealth and compounding marketers, and it drew a letter to Hims & Hers in September 2025. The convenience, price and speed angles that actually drive the click do not need it.

Why do you keep pixels off the intake page?

Because a standard pixel on a health intake form sends health-adjacent data to an ad platform — the exact conduct the FTC fined BetterHelp ($7.8M) and GoodRx ($1.5M) for under the Health Breach Notification Rule. We track conversions server-side with health-identifying fields stripped before anything is sent.

How fast does the Compliance Redline ship?

Five business days from access. You receive a redlined document covering up to three pages and ten live ads; every flagged line cites the rule and shows the corrected version beside it. The $950 is credited toward month one if you start a retainer.
